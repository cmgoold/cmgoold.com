There are a lot of people writing about AI. And while I don't particularly want to add to the noise, the guessing games, the doomsaying, I am formulating my own small notes on AI in society, in the workplace, and how I interact with it personally. I don't want to fall into reductive statements where I predict the net payoff of AI, beneficial or maladaptive, because we simply don't yet know enough, and humans are usually quite poor at making predictions. They'll probably be a few of these over time.

My use of AI, specifically generative AI, like many people's use, has changed markedly in the last year. It's went from being something I interacted with occasionally, to something that I interact with daily, 98% of the time for my work building software. I'll admit, I was resistant at first, in the same way that us humans can be stubborn to change from the status quo in other ways. I was never particularly intrigued with generative AI beyond an interest in its statistical foundations in discrete mixture modelling,

First of all, the impact of AI infrastructure on the environment is a horrible thing. Data centres the size of multiple football pitches, radiating heat into the already-too-warm atmosphere, monopolising electricity and water sources in localities which have already been degraded by their jostling to get there in the first place, are a bad thing, right now, irregardless of whether they become more efficient or they end up being a net benefit. We can only hope. For instance, if they end up being a bad thing in the way that the introduction of modern rail transport was a bad thing to many workers a couple of hundred years ago, but have ended up being an overall positive advancement, then perhaps that's okay.
